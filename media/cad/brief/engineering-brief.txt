# Niva prototype blueprint: from bench insole to health-camp kit

Draft v0.1 · 28 September 2026Scope: first buildable prototype and the experiments that gate it

This turns the Niva knee-screening concept into a prototype a small team can start building now. It recommends one architecture and says which parts rest on datasheets, which are our choices, and which need experiments. The headline: build the simplest bilateral kit that can test the sensing hypothesis, using parts we can buy in India this month. Custom PCBs and moulded housings wait until the bench and gait-lab experiments justify them.

## 1 · Recommended design and major feasibility limits

### The recommendation in one paragraph

CHOICE A **passive insole** (five force sensors plus one heel vibration film, no electronics under the foot) connects through a **flat shielded strip** to a **shin pod** built around an ESP32-C3 module, an external 12-bit ADC, a 6-axis IMU and a **removable battery cartridge** that is only ever charged in a dock. One insole and one pod per leg. The phone collects results over Bluetooth, and everything works offline.

### What the sensors measure, estimate, and cannot see

| Quantity | What it is | Niva’s relationship to it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plantar pressure** | Force per unit area at each point of the sole (kPa) | Not measured. Five discs sample five small areas. In our own StepUP-P150 analysis, a 4-position layout observed about 3.55 % of the loaded area during barefoot walking (assumed positions, walkway data). |
| **Force at a sensor** | Force over one sensor’s active area (N) | FACT Measured directly after per-sensor calibration, within each sensor’s rated range |
| **Total foot force** | The whole ground reaction force under the foot | Not measured. Unloaded regions are invisible, so any total is a model estimate |
| **Centre of pressure (CoP)** | The force-weighted average position of load under the foot | An approximation from five points. In our R2 analysis, side-to-side error (8.94 mm) was smaller than front-to-back error (16.49 mm) for a 4-position layout. The published literature advises caution at nine sensors or fewer (Fuchs et al. 2024). |
| **External knee adduction moment (KAM)** | The turning moment of the ground reaction force about the knee in the frontal plane, from inverse dynamics | EXPERIMENT Potentially estimable with a validated model. Published insoles reached r = 0.96 on their own hardware (Snyder et al. 2023). Ours is unproven. |
| **Medial knee contact force** | The actual compressive force inside the medial joint | Cannot be inferred reliably. It needs musculoskeletal models or instrumented implants. We make no claim. |
| **Screening-risk score** | A statistical output linking features to a clinical outcome | Only after reference data with X-ray or clinical labels exist, and only with external validation |

**Two modelling problems, not one.** Walking and chair-rise load the knee differently. In the 2025 insole study, knee-load prediction was strong for walking but only moderate for sit-to-stand KAM (r = 0.50; Snyder et al. 2025). We therefore use the chair-rise for repetition count, timing and left–right symmetry only, and do not report a chair-rise KAM.

### Major feasibility limits

- FACT The FSR402 in the current prototype is specified for 0.1–10 N (Interlink datasheet), far below plantar loads. A sensor rated for 445 N exists in the same form factor (FlexiForce A201, 100 lb version). Choosing between them is the first bench experiment.
- EXPERIMENT Whether a shin-only IMU plus five plantar points carries enough information for KAM is unknown. Knee frontal angle needs the thigh as well, which we do not instrument.
- EXPERIMENT Running high-impedance heel-vibration signals about 30 cm up a moving leg may pick up cable-motion noise.

## 2 · Physical architecture for chappal, sandal and shoe

One sensing insert, one strip and one shin pod serve every footwear type. Only the retention parts change.

### A · Chappal

- Insert sits on the existing footbed. A soft slot between the big-toe and second-toe regions takes the toe post.
- No trace or sensor within the toe-post zone. The big-toe sensor sits medial to the slot.
- A removable soft silicone heel keeper hooks over the chappal’s rear edge to stop the insert sliding forward.

### B · Sandal

- Insert rests on the footbed under the sandal’s own straps. No extra straps are added.
- The strip exits at the lateral heel, below the heel strap and clear of any buckle, then runs up to the pod.
- The same heel keeper is used if the sandal has no heel strap.

### C · Shoe

- CHOICE Replace the removable sockliner where possible. Sitting on top raises the foot and may make the shoe too tight.
- ASSUMPTION Not every shoe has a removable liner or enough volume. Those get the barefoot oversole instead.
- The flat strip passes the lateral heel and exits at the collar through a soft sleeve. Nothing rigid sits at the heel.

### Gumboots

A gumboot shaft covers the lower shin where the pod sits. CHOICE For testing, gumboot wearers use the barefoot oversole. This also removes a footwear type that varies a lot.

### Barefoot

A washable rubber traction oversole with a shallow pocket holds the same insert. The patient walks in it like a thin slipper. It doubles as the standard condition for anyone whose footwear does not fit the insert.

### Shin pod

Rounded pod in a shaped cradle on a broad, smooth silicone ladder strap, above the ankle bones on the lateral shin. Starting envelope 48 × 34 × 13 mm CHOICE, revised once the battery cartridge is sized. One button and one status light. Small NIVA wordmark only.

**Footwear changes the measurement.** The sole, cushioning and fit between foot and insert all differ. Our own data shows how much: barefoot and shod walkway conditions gave different coverage for the same layout (3.55 % vs 2.18 % of area, R2). CHOICE Footwear type is recorded for every test and treated as a model covariate. Any knee-load model is validated separately per footwear group before its outputs are pooled.

**Bilateral first.** A unilateral kit halves the sensor and pod cost, but it loses left–right comparisons: step-timing asymmetry, chair-rise loading symmetry, and any knee-load estimate for the other leg. CHOICE Unilateral stays a later low-cost option, to be decided on pilot data.

## 3 · Insole layer stack and sensor placement

| Layer (top to bottom) | Material | Thickness | Status |

| Comfort / load-spreading | Closed-cell PU foam | ≈ 1.0 mm | EXPERIMENT changes sensor response; calibrate as built |
| Sensor layer | FlexiForce A201 sensors | 0.203 mm | FACT Tekscan A201 datasheet |
| Heel vibration film | PVDF film on polyester, e.g. TE LDT0-028K class | 28 µm + 0.125 mm | FACT TE LDT datasheet; crimp-free variant to select |
| Base with routing channels | EVA or TPU sheet, sensor pockets and tail channels cut in | ≈ 1.0 mm | CHOICE |
| Edge seal | Heat-welded TPU perimeter | — | CHOICE |
| **Total** |  | ≈ 2.6 mm + adhesive | EXPERIMENT measure on the built part |

### Placement and routing

- Five sensing regions: big toe, medial forefoot (1st metatarsal head), lateral forefoot (5th metatarsal head), lateral midfoot, heel centre. The PVDF film sits under the heel beside the heel force sensor.
- CHOICE Positions come from anatomical landmarks scaled to each size template, then get checked against footprints of pilot participants. They are not drawn by eye.
- FACT The A201 comes with a 191 mm tail (trim options down to 51 mm) ending in 3 rigid square pins. The long tail lets every rigid pin and joint sit **outside** the insole outline, at the strip junction above the heel.
- Tails run in recessed channels along the lightly loaded margin of the insole, so they do not make a ridge. Nothing is soldered under the foot.
- **Sizes:** a small set of size templates, each built as its own part. Trimming is allowed only in a sensor-free toe margin, marked with printed trim lines. The number of sizes will be set from measured foot lengths.

**Workshop method (now):** laser- or knife-cut foam and EVA, sensors placed in pockets, contact adhesive, TPU top film heat-sealed with an iron and a PTFE sheet. **Production method (later):** a custom printed flexible circuit with integrated sensing areas, RF-welded TPU envelope, die-cut foams.

## 5 · Preliminary connections and pin allocation

### Analog or digital down the strip?

CHOICE **Analog for v0.** A passive insole keeps every chip off the foot, which is the hardest safety rule. Force signals are slow and low-impedance, so they tolerate ≈ 30 cm of cable well. The piezo signal is the risk: it is high-impedance, and cable flexing can generate charge (triboelectric noise). EXPERIMENT E3 measures this. If it fails, v1 moves a tiny buffer and ADC onto the strip above the shoe collar, outside the foot, and the strip turns digital.

| Strip pin | Signal | Pod ADC channel | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | VEXC | CH6 (sense) | Switched excitation; measured every block (ratiometric check) |
| 2–6 | FSR1–FSR5 | CH0–CH4 | Big toe, medial forefoot, lateral forefoot, lateral midfoot, heel |
| 7 | PZ+ | CH5 via buffer | Twisted with pin 8 where the strip allows |
| 8 | PZ− / AGND | — | Piezo return |
| 9 | ID | CH7 | Resistor in the insole encodes side and size |
| 10 | GND / shield drain | — | Shield bonded at the pod end only |

- **Strip build (v0):** 10-way flat flexible cable laminated in TPU, with copper-foil shielding over the piezo pair, in a teal silicone sleeve. Strain relief at both ends. Bend radius and retention clips follow the cable maker’s minimum (part to select).
- **Connector:** a keyed, gasketed connector at the pod. Left and right use different keys. The insole, strip and pod-side connector are one replaceable assembly.
- **Cable-motion artifacts:** the strip is clipped to the strap so it cannot swing. A fixed test motion is run during quality control.

**Runtime.** ASSUMPTION The cartridge cell is 300 mAh, as a planning figure. Recording alone at 35 mA then gives about 8.6 h. A camp day needs recording, BLE transfer and idle time between patients. The real figure is measured in Stage 2, with a spare cartridge per pod as the fallback.

| Stream | Rate | Bytes/s | Why this rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 force channels | 200 Hz each | 2,000 | CHOICE Twice the 100 Hz used by StepUP-P150 and our plan, giving margin for heel-strike rise times |
| IMU, 6 axes | ≈ 200 Hz | 2,400 | CHOICE Nearest supported IMU output rate; orientation filtering |
| Heel PVDF | 2,000 Hz | 4,000 | EXPERIMENT Deliberately high for v0 so the pilot data’s spectrum can set the final rate. Later, store only a window around each heel strike |
| **Total** |  | **≈ 8,400** | A 120 s recording is about 1.0 MB. A 2 MB flash partition holds about 4 min, so data transfers after each patient |

**ADC load:** about 3,000 conversions/s against the MCP3208’s 50 ksps at 2.7 V. **Sync:** the right pod broadcasts an ESP-NOW beacon with its timestamp. The left pod records each beacon’s arrival time and fits offset and drift by linear regression. A double-stomp at the start and end of each test gives an independent alignment check. EXPERIMENT E7 measures the residual.

## 7 · Firmware and phone workflow

```
SLEEP ──button──▶ WAKE_SELFTEST ──ok──▶ PAIRING ──▶ SIDE_ATTACH_CHECK ──▶ CALIBRATION
  ▲                   │fail                               │mismatch        │quiet stand
  │                   ▼                                   ▼                ▼
  └──timeout──── FAULT (red) ◀────────────────────── FAULT (red)         READY
                                                                          │start (phone)
                     QUALITY_ASSESS ◀──stop── RECORDING ◀─────────────────┘
                       │green / amber / red
                       ▼
                   RESULT_TRANSFER ──done──▶ READY ──idle timeout──▶ SLEEP
```

```
// acquisition + quality control, per pod (sketch)
on IMU_FIFO_WATERMARK: read IMU block, stamp with local µs clock
every 5 ms:  EXC_EN=1; read CH0..CH4, CH6; EXC_EN=0        // force + excitation sense
every 0.5 ms: read CH5                                     // heel PVDF
on ESPNOW_BEACON(t_right): store (t_right, t_local)        // sync pairs
every 1 s: read CH7 (ID), fuel gauge
append blocks to flash ring; on flash write error → FAULT

QUALITY_ASSESS(recording):
  flags = []
  if any sample gap or FIFO overflow            → flags += REPEAT
  if CH6 outside calibrated band                → flags += RECAL      // excitation or moisture fault
  if FSR zero-load offset drifted beyond calibration band → flags += RECAL
  if fraction of FSR samples at ADC ceiling > pre-set limit → flags += REPEAT (saturation)
  if quiet-stand shank inclination before vs after differs > pre-set limit → flags += REPEAT (strap slip)
  if ID resistor ≠ side/size set at pairing     → flags += RECAL
  if left/right step counts disagree            → flags += REPEAT
  if sync fit residual > pre-set limit          → flags += REPEAT
  status = RED if RECAL in flags, AMBER if REPEAT in flags, else GREEN
  // limits are set from Stage 1 bench data and declared before any patient testing
```

### Phone workflow (offline)

1. **Patient**: study ID only, no name. Consent is recorded on the phone. Nothing uploads without a separate, explicit consent.
2. **Fit**: pictures show the L/R insole and the pod on the outer shin. The app confirms side from each pod’s ID resistor.
3. **Calibrate**: sit with feet unloaded (zero), then quiet stand (reference inclination).
4. **Walk test** → quality result: ✓ “Good recording”, ↻ “Repeat this test”, ⚠ “Recalibrate the sensor”. Every colour comes with a symbol and text.
5. **Chair-rise test** → the same quality screen.
6. **Save**: results are stored encrypted on the phone and exported later.

Calibration coefficients carry a version number and date, and are stored on both the pod and the phone. Every recording references the calibration version it used.

## 8 · Calibration and signal-processing approach

1. **Raw-signal inspection**: plot every channel from the first recordings before any processing.
2. **Calibration**: per sensor, conditioned first, then a multi-point load/unload curve through a puck matched to the sensing area (the team’s SOP-01). Repeated at in-shoe temperature, because the A201 datasheet specifies 0.36 %/°C.
3. **Events**: heel strike and toe-off from the force channels, cross-checked against the PVDF heel transient; chair-rise phases from shank inclination.
4. **Features**: stance and swing times, cadence, side-to-side CoP proxy, heel-to-forefoot loading ratios, left–right symmetry, shank angular velocity.
5. **Quality checks**: as in section 7. Any flagged segment is excluded from analysis.
6. **Exploratory biomechanics**: KAM regression trained against gait-lab reference data.
7. **Screening model**: only once reference data with clinical labels exist.

**Limits of sparse sensing.** Five points cannot know the contact area or the load between sensors, and each sensor is nonlinear and hysteretic (FSR402 hysteresis +10 %, datasheet). Any total force or CoP from five points is therefore a model output with its own error. That error must be reported, never presented as a measurement.

| Reference measurement | Validates | Still model-dependent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calibrated weights / loading fixture | Each sensor’s force response, drift, temperature effect | Behaviour inside the shoe |
| Force plate | Event timing, total vertical force, CoP estimate | Knee moments |
| Force plate + 3D motion capture (gait lab) | KAM estimate via inverse dynamics | Medial contact force |
| Instrumented knee implant data (published) | Only the modelling chain, on other people | Anything about our users |
| X-ray grade and clinical exam | Association between features and OA status | Progression, which needs follow-up |

No clinical threshold, sensitivity, specificity or accuracy figure is stated anywhere in this plan. They can only come from a pre-registered study with an independent reference standard, followed by validation at a different site.

## 9 · Mechanical safety, charging exclusion and left/right controls

### Nothing rigid under the sole

- All pins and joints sit outside the insole outline (A201 tail length makes this possible).
- **Hard-spot test:** stand on the insole over pressure-indicating film. Any local mark fails the part. Repeated after cyclic ageing (SOP-02).
- Thickness mapped with a micrometer on a grid, new and aged.
- Pilot protocol: skin check after first wear; participants with an existing foot ulcer are excluded.

### Charging requires removal

- The pod has no charging socket, no coil and no charger.
- The cartridge release sits on the pod’s back face and is covered by the cradle while the pod is worn.
- The dock slot fits the bare cartridge only; the assembled pod is physically too large to mate.
- Cartridge contacts are recessed. BQ2970 protection plus the dock’s NTC check form two independent layers.
- **Test:** 10 attempts to charge an assembled or worn pod must all fail.

### Left and right

- Mirrored insole outlines, moulded L/R letters and tactile dots.
- Different connector keys, so a left insole cannot plug into a right pod.
- Each insole carries an ID resistor, and each pod stores its side. A mismatch means RED.
- **Not prevented:** a complete matched left kit worn on the right leg. The fitting check covers this: “pod on the outer side of the leg, wordmark facing out”.

**Foreseeable misuse and failure modes:** cartridge crushed or punctured (hard shell, protection IC, no charging on the body); strip pulled hard (strain relief and a breakaway connector at the pod); pod worn on bare skin in heat (smooth rounded shell, no charging heat source); a user cleaning it with solvents (cleaning card, material testing in section 11).

## 11 · Assembly, repair and cleaning

- **Replaceable parts:** insole + strip assembly, silicone strap, cradle, battery cartridge, heel keeper, oversole. The pod is screw-assembled with a gasket. No glue-sealed electronics.
- **Materials:** TPU and silicone on every surface the patient touches. Nothing absorbent, and no fabric strap that holds sweat. The strap is a smooth silicone ladder design with no trapped seams.
- **Cleaning between patients:** wipe the insole top, strap and pod with a hospital wipe. The oversole is washed.
- **Cleaning validation plan:** repeated wipe cycles with each candidate disinfectant (for example 70 % isopropyl alcohol) on material coupons and on built insoles. Check swelling, delamination, adhesive failure and sensor calibration shift. No disinfectant compatibility or IP rating is claimed until tested.

| E6 | A health worker can fit it in under a minute | Timed fitting by people who did not build it, with only the picture card |
| E7 | Two pods stay in sync | Simultaneous double-stomp across repeated sessions; measure alignment residual |

Start with E1, E3 and E5. They need no participants and no new hardware beyond an A201 sample set, and each one can change the design before money goes into anything else.
