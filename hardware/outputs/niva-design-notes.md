# NIVA — physical design concept

Photorealistic concept images, generated with the built-in image-generation tool. These depict a proposed physical design, not an engineered or clinically validated device.

## Form and attachment
A compact rounded shin pod in pale grey-blue, with navy sides, one teal button and a single three-colour status light. Nominal envelope: 48 × 34 × 13 mm, subject to component packaging. A broad, smooth silicone ladder strap and shaped cradle locate the motion sensor on the lower shin above the ankle bones. The cradle has a defined orientation; calibrate after fitting.

A roughly 2.5–3 mm soft foot-shaped insert rests on the sandal footbed, underneath the foot. A toe-web slot accommodates rubber chappal posts, with sensor traces routed around it. A soft heel loop and removable low-profile instep keeper retain it where needed. In closed shoes and gumboots, the same insert fits inside and the flat tail routes up the leg; use only where there is sufficient clearance, without pressure at boot collars. For barefoot patients, place the insert in the supplied washable rubber grip sole with soft adjustable straps.

Offer size-matched left/right inserts rather than cut-to-size sensor sheets. Target fitting sequence: select size, seat inserts, close heel keepers, strap pods and confirm side/orientation on the phone. Under-one-minute fitting is a design target requiring timed field trials.

## Soft sensing surface
Five flexible pressure elements lie beneath the big toe, medial and lateral forefoot, lateral midfoot and heel. A thin flexible piezoelectric vibration film lies beneath the heel. Laminate them on flexible traces within a sealed TPU envelope between continuous soft closed-cell cushioning layers. No solder joints, plugs, battery, rigid circuit board or locally raised sensor bosses lie beneath the plantar surface. Heel vibration-film depth and cushioning require signal and pressure-distribution testing. Nothing electronic is visible through the top surface.

## Protected connection and service
An overmolded flat flexible tail exits the lateral heel and runs close to the ankle, retained at the shin strap. Rolled slack sits in a shallow cradle channel. Strain relief protects both ends. The covered connector stays above the foot. The insole and its tail replace together; the pods, straps, cradle and battery cartridges replace independently.

Pods use simple two-piece molded PC/ABS shells, service screws accessible off the body, and a replaceable perimeter gasket. Use common electronics and battery formats within serviceable cartridges. Soft parts use molded silicone, TPU and closed-cell foam. Avoid fabric sleeves, absorbent foam trays and exposed hook-and-loop fasteners. Validate chosen cleaning agents against every material and seal before specifying a disinfection procedure or ingress rating.

## Charging physically separated from wearing
There is no USB socket, inductive charger or charging circuit in either wearable pod. The battery is a sealed cartridge. Its release is on the rear of the pod and physically covered by the strapped cradle, so the pod must first be unclipped. Removing the cartridge disconnects the wearable. Only a removed cartridge fits the recessed charging wells in the case; a whole pod cannot fit. Dock contacts are shrouded inside the wells. The dock accepts USB power from a wall supply or power bank. Validate this mechanical exclusion with prototypes.

## Left/right control
Mirrored foot shapes, toe-web slots, heel cups and large molded L/R marks guide fitting. Left and right insole plugs and cradle keys use different, physically incompatible shapes; colour alone never determines side. Pod identity and insole side are checked electronically. The fitting screen includes a worker-guided right-foot lift confirmation and blocks test start if the reporting side is wrong. Mechanical keys prevent crossed assembly; correct placement on a person's anatomy still needs a fitting check.

## Health-camp use
Bluetooth control from the worker's own phone, with local storage and later synchronisation, is the proposed software behaviour. Green means valid acquisition, amber repeat, red recalibrate; none is a disease-risk colour. The phone also shows words and symbols.

The base kit contains paired shin units, size options and spare inserts, barefoot grip soles, spare straps, removable batteries, a charging cradle, USB cable and power bank in a gasketed polypropylene case with a removable smooth tray. The phone shown belongs to the worker. The optional knee-sound sensor is omitted from this base design to keep cost, cleaning and fitting effort down.

Image dimensions and small markings are illustrative. Prototype checks should cover pressure hotspots, traction, footwear clearance, fit time, left/right misuse, sensor calibration, signal quality and cleaning durability. Clinical screening accuracy and referral thresholds need separate validation.
